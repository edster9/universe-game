# Time passing while a player is away

Recorded 2026-09-29 **as a placeholder for a discussion the owner has asked for**, before the [vocabulary](vocabulary.md) conversation and the other planned discussions. Nothing here is decided. The requirement is "Time passing while a player is away" in [requirements.md](../requirements.md).

## The question

The world keeps running when a player logs off. Their person still burns food and water, still gets tired, and still stands somewhere other people and animals can reach. In the challenges so far a person who stops acting dies of thirst in about three and a half days. So what happens to your person while you're away, as the rest of the world sees it?

## Why it's hard here

- **Nothing from nowhere.** A person can't be kept alive for free: food and water have to come from somewhere.
- **The world is shared.** Others can see, meet, rob, help, or harm an absent player's person.
- **Time is real in the world.** Crops grow, fires go out, and boars move whether or not anyone is watching.

## Directions other games are known for, to look at properly

A sourced survey is in [research/offline-players.md](../research/offline-players.md). The starting list of patterns was:

- **The body stays, asleep and vulnerable.** Some survival games leave a sleeping character in the world where they logged off, which others can find.
- **The character leaves the world.** Many games simply remove the character until the player returns, sometimes only in safe places (inns, beds, homes).
- **A safe place and a grace period.** Logging off takes effect only after a short delay, or only counts as safe somewhere protected.
- **Upkeep paid in advance.** Stored food, rent, or supplies are consumed while away, so preparation matters.
- **Someone else looks after you.** A household, a colony, or hired help keeps the person fed.
- **Time for the absent person runs differently.** The person is set aside and doesn't age or hunger while away.

## Questions for the discussion

- Does an absent person stay in the world, and can others interact with them?
- Who or what feeds them, and is that food real, taken from stores?
- Is there a safe way to leave, and what makes a place safe?
- What happens to what they carry and own?
- How does this change as people live in colonies, towns, and ships?
- How does it interact with [many universes](universes.md), and with a persistent world that keeps running on servers?

## Decided, 2026-09-29

Decided with the owner, after the [survey](../research/offline-players.md). Their words are in "Time passing while a player is away" in [requirements.md](../requirements.md).

- **Your body stays in the world, and things can happen to it.** There's no vanishing and no lingering timer: leaving in the middle of a fight means dying in it. That's the price, and it ends combat logging.
- **Safety is something you make in the world**: a locked home, a rented room while your money lasts (then eviction), a ship parked somewhere quiet in a nebula. Nothing from nowhere: rent is paid from real money, and runs out.
- **When you leave, your person becomes an NPC under standing orders you set**: a profile of how cautious or bold to be, such as keep a low profile, stay in shelter, flee trouble, or defend yourself. It runs on the same instinct machinery as the boars, and tries to stay alive as best it can.
- **You get told how your person is doing**, or that they've died, by push notification or email.
- **Needs follow the universe's rules.** In the usual universe players don't need food or sleep, so an absent person doesn't starve; in a realistic universe they live on real stores.
- **To tackle first:** protecting yourself, and setting your standing orders. Enhanced as we go.

Later: players who stay away for years, and what of theirs keeps working while they're gone (fields, furnaces, hired help).

Open, for the technology plan: simulating many absent people costs server time; quiet places may need to run more coarsely than busy ones, without breaking the laws.

## Dying, and starting again

A first take, 2026-09-29, from the owner's direction ("Dying, and starting again" in [requirements.md](../requirements.md)). Not yet decided in detail.

How others are commonly known to do it (not surveyed with sources, unlike the offline survey): World of Warcraft makes you a ghost who runs back to your body, losing almost nothing. EVE Online destroys your ship and what's fitted to it, leaves part of it for others to loot, and wakes you in a clone at your home station with your skills and hangars intact, with insurance paying back part of the ship. Rust, DayZ, and ARK take everything you carried and wake you on a beach or at your bed, with your base standing (DayZ also resets your character). Albion Online and Ultima Online leave your body to be looted but keep your skills. Permadeath games take everything.

The proposed balance, the owner's with one refinement:

1. **Knowledge comes back with you**: skills, practice, recognition, and your memory of places. That's what took real time to earn.
2. **What was on your body stays with the body**, where you fell, for others to take or for you to recover if you get back first.
3. **What you left somewhere safe stays yours**: a home, a docked ship, stores, a bank account. You lose what you risked by carrying it, not everything, which gives banks, storage, and insurance a real purpose.
4. **The new body comes from somewhere**, since nothing comes from nowhere: a clone bay or a medical bay in the space age, made of real matter and paid for, perhaps by insurance; waking on the beach again in a stone-age tutorial.
5. **Each universe chooses how harsh it is**, from forgiving, through this default, to permadeath.
