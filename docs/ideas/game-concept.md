# Game concept

Working picture, rewritten 2026-09-28. This is the game that eventually runs on the [world engine](world-engine.md). It is the destination, not the first attempt. The first attempt is [slices.md](../slices.md).

## The hour of play

1. You are in a ship, in a small set of systems you can learn by name.
2. You buy goods, haul them, fight or run, and dock.
3. Docking or landing ends the flight simulator and starts the walk. The port is a place: people, shops, a mission board, a shipyard, other players if they are there.
4. You take work, spend money, hear something, and leave. The next system is a choice about who and what is there.

Money is the thread. Because the world engine conserves everything, every trade has a real other side. The shop's stock came from somewhere, the mission's payout comes from someone's budget, and the guard at the gate is paid.

## The story that shows the whole design

A gang raids a town. The guards fight back because they are paid to, and they are only as many as the town can afford. The raid succeeds, but the gang's ships are destroyed in the fight. Now they are stranded.

Getting off the planet means going down the ladder. They salvage what's left of the wrecks, find or dig ore, smelt it, and rebuild. Parts are not enough: someone has to know how to build a ship. They find a manual, or hire (or coerce) an engineer, or work it out together. Meanwhile the town they raided is short of money and stock. It recovers slowly, through traders, migrants, and aid that someone paid for.

Every step of that story is the world engine doing its job. None of it is scripted.

## The climb, and many universes

The game runs many universes (see [universes.md](universes.md)). Some start in the space age, some are free-for-all, and some will destroy themselves. Every one faces the same challenge, whether it starts there or falls back to it: the climb from a desert island to a chip fab, to teleportation, and beyond (see [production.md](production.md)). A universe that survives and climbs furthest is the ultimate experience.

## Why the cut between flight and walking stays

A continuous world from orbit to a city street is what Star Citizen has spent more than a decade on. This game switches presentation on purpose:

- In space, the body that matters is the ship: instruments, thrust, weapons, docking.
- On foot, the body that matters is the person: conversation, contracts, the market floor, the forge.

The cut can be visible. What must survive it is the player: the same cargo, cash, reputation, knowledge, and obligations.

## Why the map stays small

Elite Dangerous and No Man's Sky won the argument about scale and lost the argument about meeting people. This game wants another player to be a normal event in a port, a market, on a route, or in a fight.

A small map also pays for the world engine. Deep simulation of every shop, guard, and furnace is only affordable if there aren't billions of them.

How small "small" is remains open. The test is social: can two strangers end up in the same market without arranging it?

## What this is closest to

| Already exists | What it covers | What it leaves open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Classic Elite, Oolite | Ship, trade, combat, docking, missions | Walking a place; an economy with real sources |
| Kenshi, Mount & Blade | Towns you can raid, guards and garrisons, consequences | Space; building from materials |
| Dwarf Fortress, Wurm Online, Vintage Story | Materials, smelting, forging, an engine that doesn't know "sword" | Space; a shared trading universe |
| X4, EVE Online | An economy where ships come from supply chains | Walking a port; building from raw materials by hand |
| Star Citizen | Flying and walking one universe | A scope a small project can finish |

No game owns the combination: a conserved world engine, knowledge as an input, a small shared map, and an in-game computer.

## Out of scope until someone adds it

A story campaign, procedural galaxies, seamless atmospheric flight, and fleet command. Building and survival are no longer out of scope: the stranded crew is the showcase.
